# Same-identity member rewind

Status: draft for review, fifth revision. This note supplies the design note that the disposition of #2117 asked for before same-identity rewind is built. Same-identity rewind is deferred: it is not part of the current release, and no implementation is expected against this note until it is selected. The note settles the contract. It is docs only: no API, wire type or test changes, and `a_member_session_forks_at_a_message_boundary` in `crates/meerkat-rpc/tests/member_session_fork_at_boundary.rs` stays `#[ignore]`d.

Source baseline: Meerkat `main` at `ad31c1fd31517c0c1908084fae459758808cfd2d`. Paths and names below describe that baseline. The third revision restated the contract in the engine's own terms: the rewind is a third rotation target, not a new seat transition. This revision closes the maintainer's second review: a fork preflight at Begin and a defined end for a failure after the start row, the fork's instruction policy by name, recovery that observes the planned child instead of forking again, the turn window, an incarnation-change stream item with its wire shape, a redo boundary drawn by terminal kind instead of creation id, and the tool grants, session read and key file of the archived session. The fifth revision closes the third review: source resolution guarded by the fork's prefix digest so a compaction cannot shift the boundary, the incarnation-change item and rotation-following streams made opt-in on `mob/stream_open` with the item on its own notification method, peer messages in the door table, and the smaller points (the start's same-session shape, the key carry after verification, the digest over the normalized prefix, a read-only lifted fork read, the input guard by name, a bounded chain cleared at a mob reset).

## Problem

A host that runs an interactive agent as a durable mob member wants "rewind": the user picks an earlier message, and the member's own conversation continues from that boundary. The continuation must have the exact context (the prefix byte for byte, including the copied system prompt), keep the same identity, and record where it came from.

Today none of the member surfaces gives that:

- `mob/fork_member` (`MobHandle::fork_member`) copies the exact prefix, but seats it under a **new** identity. A fork onto the source identity is refused before anything is forked: `admit_fork_member` returns `MobError::MemberAlreadyExists` when `member.identity == source_identity` (pinned over RPC by `mob_fork_member_onto_a_seated_identity_commits_nothing`).
- `session/fork_at` refuses a member's session with `INVALID_PARAMS`, "mob-owned session transcripts cannot be edited through the generic session surface" (`session_fork_at_refuses_a_member_session`). The same refusal covers `session/fork_replace`, `session/rewrite_transcript` and `session/restore_transcript_revision`.
- Re-seating the identity on a fork child's session is refused with `data.kind = resume_session_bound_to_another_member` (`main_cannot_be_reseated_on_another_members_session`): the child's session is durably bound to the child's identity at fork commit, and `crates/meerkat-mob/src/build.rs` refuses a resume whose identity does not match the stored binding.

The consumer is rkat-tui's `/rewind` in attach mode: one durable host-root member `main` per workspace, driven over the daemon. The user picks an earlier message, and `main` continues from that boundary.

## Requirements

1. **Exact prefix.** The continued transcript is the source's messages before `message_index`, including the copied system prompt.
2. **Same identity continues.** After the rewind, `main` answers. `mob/member_status` and every member-targeted method resolve `main` to the rewound conversation.
3. **Lineage recorded.** The source session and boundary are durable and readable.
4. **Inherited restrictions.** The member keeps its tool access policy, dispatch admission and instruction policy.
5. **Idle source only.** A member that owes work is refused typed. Nothing is started, forked or rebound.
6. **Durable.** After a restart the member resumes on the rewound conversation; a crash at any point finishes the rotation, forward or at its defined failure end.
7. **Redo.** The previous tail stays readable through `session/history`, and the host can continue the member from it again.

## Options

### (A) Re-seat the identity on a prefix fork

Fork into a staging identity, retire `main`, rewrite the child's persisted binding to `main`. Rejected: it needs the binding rewrite that `ResumedIdentityRejection::bound_to_another_member` exists to refuse, spans two identities and three journal facts, and is unnecessary because `DurableSessionForkTarget::member_binding` binds the child to any identity at commit.

### (B) A third rotation target: a committed fork of the identity's own session

The identity rotates onto a durable fork of its own session, born bound to the same identity, through the machinery `rotate_member` and `respawn_member` already use. The contract is below.

### (C) New identity, host follows

`mob/fork_member` and let the host switch to `main-2`. Right for forks; it does not meet "same identity", and identity churn reaches the spawn customizer, grants, wiring, status consumers and the host's conversation model.

### (D) Same-session rewind through the transcript revision graph

A mob-owned `session/rewrite_transcript` that cuts `[message_index, end)`; redo is `session/restore_transcript_revision`. Its strength is real: everything keyed to the session stays in place, with no seat change and no new session id. Its costs, as the maintainer assessed them, are open-ended:

- a mob-owned transcript edit authority that does not exist (there is no `RewriteTranscript` scope: `crates/meerkat-mob/src/control_policy.rs`);
- mob-scoped revision reads, since the generic ones refuse member sessions (`router.rs`);
- a reconciliation of state keyed to transcript positions: job outcomes owed to the session, memory projections, instruction activation rows in the cut tail, and members forked at later boundaries.

### Recommendation

**(B), in rotation shape.** The maintainers have settled (B) as the direction. (B)'s costs (the dormant seat, comms identity, the fence on the old session, owed work) each map onto something that exists, and (B) leaves the old conversation intact as a whole session for history and redo.

## Proposed contract for (B)

**Method:** `mob/rewind_member`, replacing the ignored test's stand-in call.

```
MobRewindMemberParams  { mob_id, agent_identity, message_index, source_session_id? }
MobRewindMemberResult  { agent_identity, member_ref, session_id, source_session_id, message_count, generation }
```

`message_index` has its `session/fork_at` meaning. The source is the member's current session unless `source_session_id` names a session of the identity's rewind chain (see **Redo**); the result names the source the boundary was resolved to (see **Source resolution**). There is no `inherited_instructions` field: `mob/fork_member` deliberately has none. A rewind is the same member continuing, so the fork runs under **`ForkInstructionPolicy::ReactivateInherited`**, for activated keys and for skill loads alike: the child re-activates, before it commits, every key that was effective in the source **at the boundary**, not at the source's end. Keys activated in the cut tail are not carried; the copied system prompt is the prefix's. The successor's builds then apply the member's own instruction policy as for any rebuild.

**Owner and authority:** `MobHandle::rewind_member` in `crates/meerkat-mob/src/runtime/handle.rs`, admitted under `ControlScope::Retire` (a rewind retires the current incarnation, as rotations and respawn do), through `admit_member_host_door(Retire, identity)`. Under that door rule an identity entry's member can be rewound only by a principal that also holds `AdminHost`, which its host does; any other principal is refused `MobError::IdentityMember`. No agent-facing tool reaches it: it is not in the mob tool catalog, and the agent lane never acts on an identity entry's member.

**Rotation target.** `RotationTarget::CommittedFork` joins `FreshSession` and `SameSession`. Its record, `MemberForkRotationRecord { planned_session_id, source_session_id, message_count, expected_prefix, successor }`, is stored on the start row under a **new key** (`fork_rotation`), never in the existing record's key, because a release that supports rollback to 0.9.8 must not write a record 0.9.8 cannot decode (`rotation_intent.rs`). `expected_prefix` is the evidence the planned child must show: `{ prefix_message_count, prefix_digest, tool_access_policy, auth_binding, realm_id }`, the fields `PlannedChildEvidence` carries. `verify_planned_child` does not compare the digest today, so the rewind's verification compares all of them, the digest included. The fork normalizes inline images in the child before it commits (`preflight_durable_fork_blobs`), so the recorded digest is over the **normalized** prefix: Begin runs that same preflight on a clone of the source's prefix (it is also the blob preflight) and takes `transcript_prefix_digest(message_count)` of the clone, which is what the child's own digest gives.

**Start shape.** The `CommittedFork` start writes what the same-session start writes, with the member's current session `S0` in it: the rotation session `S0`, the retire-pending session `S0`, the archive-only marker, the started role (the member's own) and no peer endpoint. It adds only the target, the planned child id and `expected_prefix`. The commit moves the binding to the child; the failure end (below) changes only the target, and the same-session terminal's guards then hold as they do after a respawn's start. A rollback fixture pins what the older reader does with the record: a start row it reads as a plain retirement, a terminal pair it reads as an ordinary retirement and spawn (the successor's `rewound_from` is ignored).

### Source resolution

A boundary inside a rewound session's copied prefix is read in the session it was copied from, but only while that prefix is still the copied one. Each chain entry (see **Redo**) keeps the fork's `expected_prefix` digest. When `message_index` is at or before the entry's `message_count` for the source, admission computes the source's `transcript_prefix_digest(message_count)` and compares it with the entry's digest:

- **Equal**: the source still starts with the copied prefix, so index k names the earlier session's message k. Admission moves the boundary to the entry's `source_session_id` and repeats there. Only in the earlier session are the boundary's instruction heads the source's own, which `ReactivateInherited` requires (a head copied from an earlier session is `NotEffectiveInSource`).
- **Different, or no entry** (a compaction rebuilt the history from a summary and the retained turns, so index k now names something else; the source has fewer than `message_count` messages; the entry was evicted): resolution stops. The rewind forks the source as it is, at `message_index`, with its own typed refusals (a copied head is `NotEffectiveInSource`, a compacted head body `HeadBodyRetired`). It never maps an index across a rewrite.

The result's `source_session_id` names the session the boundary resolved to. A boundary inside a fork's re-activation suffix is refused by the preflight.

### Sequence

1. **Admission** (no state change): the scope, the source resolution and its lineage check (**Redo**), the boundary check (`message_index` is a message boundary at or before the source's committed end), and the refusals in the owed-work table. A refusal here writes nothing.
2. **Begin** = the rotation start, one actor step:
   1. Stage the mob-owned hold on the current session `S0` (`RunStartHoldReason::MemberRewind`, a new mob-owned reason, as `HostPreparation` is). Unlike a plain run-start hold it also **refuses new input** on `S0`, and the same step closes `S0`'s live comms route to new envelopes, so a peer's send takes the member's wake route (see **Admission of turns**).
   2. Under the hold, re-check `S0` idle (no run in flight, no non-terminal input in its ledger, no envelope waiting in its inbox) and run the **fork preflight** (below) against the resolved source at `message_count`.
   3. Compute `expected_prefix` over the normalized prefix the preflight returned, which is the prefix the fork commits (see **Rotation target**).
   4. `BeginMemberRotation` with target `CommittedFork`: record the intent in the same-session start's shape (**Start shape**) plus the planned child id and `expected_prefix`, mark the incarnation `Retiring`, and write the start row `MemberRetirementStarted` with the record.

   If 2.2 refuses, release the hold, reopen the live route and answer the refusal; nothing is written. The pending state is the rotation intent plus `Retiring`; there is no separate `member_rewind_pending` field.
3. **Fork** (outside the actor): `fork_durable_session_with_planned_identity` of the source at `message_count`, into the planned id, with target `DurableSessionForkTarget { member_binding: main's MobMemberBinding }`, policies `ReactivateInherited`, and no tool-access-policy argument (the child keeps the source's effective policy, as the forked participant's does). The mob seam passes no target today (`fork_planned_child` in `session_service.rs`), so the rewind needs a seam that carries the member-binding target; without it the child is born unbound. The child `S1` is born bound to `main`. Then observe and **verify** `S1` against `expected_prefix` and the member binding, exactly as recovery does (below); a mismatch takes the failure end. Only a verified child gets the comms key pair (see **Comms identity**), so an abandoned child never holds the member's key.
4. **Retire `S0`'s incarnation**: release its runtime (it is idle), settle `S0`'s owed compaction projections cold as the fresh-session rotation does (`settle_member_owed_compaction_projections`), end `S0`'s session-scoped tool grants explicitly (`end_tool_grants_of(ToolGrantSubject::session(S0))`, reason "member rewound"; see **What carries over**), and archive `S0` (disposal `Archived`, as a retirement does). Step 4 starts only once `S1` exists and matches `expected_prefix`.
5. **Commit** = the rotation terminal, one machine step: append `MemberRetired` and `MemberSpawned` in one batch; the successor's `MemberSpawned` carries `rewound_from { source_session_id, message_count }` and keeps the member's creation id (`MemberCreationProvenance::Successor`); seat the successor **Dormant** on `S1` at the next generation and fence, as an **existing** session: unlike the fresh-session terminal it writes neither `member_seat_session_unborn` nor `unborn_seat_sessions`, so the first wake resumes `S1` instead of creating a session; move the binding; append the chain entry (**Redo**); emit `MemberSessionBindingChanged { old_session_id: S0, new_session_id: S1 }` and the incarnation-change item (**Streams**); release the hold.
6. **Recovery**: replay re-records the intent and its `expected_prefix` from the start row (`RecoverMemberRotationIntent` gains a `CommittedFork` arm), re-stages the hold (and the closed live route) on `S0` while `S0` is not archived, and **observes the planned child** before anything else, as the planned-fork user does:
   - The child exists: verify it against `expected_prefix` and the member binding. A match continues at the key carry (the same bytes; a carry already done is observed), then step 4 (an archive already complete is observed, as `retirement_archive_already_complete` does today), then step 5. A mismatch takes the failure end.
   - The child does not exist: `S0` is not archived yet (step 4 follows the verified child), so the fork read still accepts it, and step 3 runs again under the re-staged hold.

   Recovery never forks against an archived source. An archived `S0` with no child cannot arise from the order above; it would be store damage, and the rotation then stays stuck and re-drivable with that cause, as below.

A transient store failure at any step leaves the rotation stuck and re-drivable (`redrive_retirement`), as other rotations are.

### Fork preflight

Admission checks the scope, the source and the boundary; the durable fork can still refuse on more. Begin decides each of those under the hold, before the start row, with the answer the client gets:

| Fork refusal | Preflight at Begin, under the hold | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Busy guard: an active runtime admission on the source, or its turn boundary not free within `QUIESCENT_FORK_BOUNDARY_BOUND` | the idle re-check, then the same guard taken once | `SESSION_BUSY`, `data.kind = fork_source_unavailable`, `cause = running` |
| Pending input boundary at the source's end (`refuse_quiescent_fork_of_pending_boundary`) | the same check on the source at `message_count` | the same busy answer |
| Split tool group (`fork_at_complete_boundary`) | the same boundary check on the source | the durable fork's boundary refusal, mapped as `session/fork_at` maps it |
| Instruction policy (`apply_fork_instruction_policies` under `ReactivateInherited`: `NotEffectiveInSource`, `HeadBodyRetired`, `LineageBodyBudgetExceeded`, an unreadable activation row) | the same function on a clone of the planned prefix, as the fork applies it | `ForkInstructionRefused` with its cause and keys, mapped as `session/fork_at` maps it |
| Blob verification (`preflight_transcript_fork_blobs`) | `preflight_messages_for_durable_fork` on the clone of the planned prefix, as the fork runs it (`crates/meerkat-core/src/image_content.rs`): every referenced blob is read and content-verified, an inline image is stored under its content address and its reference rewritten, a store-attested reference is rehomed. These stored blobs are the one write before the start row: content-addressed, so a rotation that never starts leaves at most an unreferenced blob and never a session row, and the fork's own pass over the same prefix finds them already stored | the fork's store error, before anything starts |
| Binding and comms-name checks before commit: the source has canonical session metadata, the member binding derives a comms name, and the child's persisted comms name equals it | read the source's metadata, derive `member_binding.comms_name()`, compare it with the source's comms name | `UnsupportedForMode` ("member session has no canonical metadata") for a source without metadata; an internal error for a comms name that does not match the binding (a corrupt binding, not a client error) |
| Comms identity root for the key carry | resolve the comms identity root and `S0`'s key path | a key present with no resolvable root: `UnsupportedForMode` ("no comms identity root to carry the member's key"); no key: nothing to carry |

With these decided and the hold refusing runs and input, the fork's remaining failures are store faults, which are transient.

### The failure end

A non-transient failure after the start row (a fork refusal the preflight did not foresee, or a planned child whose evidence conflicts with `expected_prefix`) does not leave the member `Retiring` with only the mob's destroy as an exit. The rotation finishes as a **same-session successor on `S0`**: a conflicting child is archived (as `fail_activation_and_archive` does for forked participants; it holds no key, since the carry follows verification), one machine step restates the recorded target from `CommittedFork` to `SameSession`, drops the planned child id and `expected_prefix`, and **journals the restate** as a row of its own, `MemberRotationRestated { agent_identity, generation, from: CommittedFork, to: SameSession, cause }`, appended before the terminal; `S0`'s runtime is released as for a respawn, and the existing same-session terminal (`CommitMemberRotationSameSession`, the `…Kickoff` form when one is owed) seats the member Dormant on `S0` at the next generation with its key, endpoint and edges, and releases the hold. Because the start took the same-session shape, the restate is the only fact that changes: the rotation session is `S0`, the retire-pending session is `S0` with the disposal `KeptForSuccessor`, and the started role is the member's own, which are what that terminal guards on. The failure end is reachable only before step 4, so `S0` is never archived when it is taken. The caller gets `data.kind = rewind_not_committed`, with `cause` naming the fork's refusal and the generation the member continues at; a rotation finished by recovery has no caller, and the host learns the outcome from the incarnation-change item (rotation `same_session`) and `mob/member_status`. The chain gains no entry.

**Replay reads the failure end from the journal.** Replay re-records the intent as `CommittedFork` from the start row and then applies the restate row, so the recovered intent is `SameSession` before the same-session terminal pair is read, and that terminal's guards hold as they do for a respawn. The restate is journaled rather than inferred for recovery's sake: a crash between the restate and the terminal would otherwise replay a `CommittedFork` intent, observe no planned child (the conflicting one was archived) and fork again against a source the failure end had already decided to keep. With the row, a recovery after the restate drives the same-session terminal and forks nothing; a recovery before it observes the child and decides afresh, as **Sequence** step 6 says. A `fork_rotation` start row followed by a same-session terminal pair with no restate row between them is store damage: the rotation stays stuck and re-drivable with that cause.

**No Build step.** The existing rotations build nothing: the successor is seated Dormant and its first delivery builds it. The rewind does the same, so a dormant member is not made live as a side effect, no second runtime ever claims the comms name while the old one holds it (the in-process registry would refuse it, `crates/meerkat-comms/src/inproc.rs`), and there is no `PendingProvision` to cancel: the wake path carries its own guard and rollback. The first build after the rewind resolves the dispatch admission and the per-spawn overlay through the spawn customizer, as for any rebuild (`RotationSuccessor` records neither).

**Fork-derived members cannot be rewound** in this contract. A dormant seat refuses a spec with `fork_source`, `fork_job` or a forked participant attachment, and a fork-derived member has no same-session successor. Begin refuses such a member `MobError::UnsupportedForMode` ("a fork-derived member cannot be rewound"), before anything starts, until a dormant seat can carry fork lineage. rkat-tui's `main` is host-root and unaffected.

**Autonomous members cannot be rewound.** Begin refuses any member whose runtime mode is not `TurnDriven` (`UnsupportedForMode`). An `AutonomousHost` member's loop and kickoff obligations are keyed to its session's conversation, and a rewound conversation would need a rule for re-owing them that this contract does not define.

### Comms identity

Choice: **carry the old session's key pair into the new session.** The comms key pair is stored per session (`<root>/<session>/identity.key`, `crates/meerkat-mob/src/runtime/tests/seat_comms_key.rs`), and the durable fork copies none, so without a carry `S1` gets a new key and endpoint while peers trust the old one. The two existing answers each lose something: a fresh-session successor records a new endpoint and drops the member's edges, re-wiring only declared ones (explicit `wire()` edges and external peer edges are lost); a same-session successor under its own role keeps key, endpoint and edges. The rewind takes the second answer's outcome: step 3 copies `S0`'s key pair to `S1`'s key path under the root the preflight resolved, the record names the member's existing endpoint, and the terminal keeps the member's endpoint, peer id and edges, as `CommitMemberRotationSameSession` does under the member's own role. This is safe only because `S0` is fenced: archived in step 4, so no runtime can come up on it with the same key, and its runtime was released before the successor can wake. A member whose session has no key pair (network comms before the first wake) carries none and owes its endpoint as a same-session successor does.

`S0` keeps its key file after the archive. The rewind therefore never records `S0` as an identity entry's archived session (`identity_entry_archived_sessions`): an entry archived after a rewind records its current session, `S1`, so an unarchive can never seat the identity on `S0` while `S1` holds the same key.

### Fence on the old session

`turn/start` does no owner check (`router.rs`), and a session whose mob owner no longer binds it "proceeds as before" (`crates/meerkat-rpc/src/session_runtime.rs`); session-targeted schedules (`resumable_session`, `crates/meerkat-schedule/src/tools.rs`) resume such a session too. Only archived sessions are refused. Step 4 therefore archives `S0` as part of the rotation, so after the terminal no door can run it and its ledger runs nothing. `session/history` still reads archived member sessions (`router.rs`, "archived mob-owned sessions should still route to session/history"), so requirement 7's read holds. Redo and source resolution read an archived session, so two reads that refuse archived sources today are lifted **for lineage-authorized sources only**: the fork read (`source_session_for_transcript_edit_locked`) and the session read (both `reject_if_archived_session` calls in `persistent.rs`'s `read`). The lift is a mob-internal entry that carries the chain proof; the generic fork and read surfaces keep refusing archived sources. The lifted fork read is **read-only**: where the normal fork read persists a projection it materialized from replay back onto its source (`persist_replayed_transcript_projection_for_mutation`), the lifted read keeps the projection in memory and writes nothing to the archived source.

### Owed work outside the runtime ledger

The idle re-check covers the runtime and its input ledger. Everything else is decided at Begin:

| Owed work | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| A run in flight, including one waiting on an approval decision | Refuse: `SESSION_BUSY`, `data.kind = fork_source_unavailable`, `cause = running`. A run blocked on an approval is a run in flight. |
| Detached and `fork_off` jobs whose outcomes are owed to `S0` (`ForkJobTerminalEvent::owner_session_id`, `detached_jobs` on `mob/member_status`) | Refuse, the same busy refusal (retryable once the jobs end). Carrying would need a re-owning of outcomes to `S1` whose prefix never saw the dispatch. |
| Owed compaction projections of `S0` | Carry by settling: step 4 settles them cold before the archive, as the fresh-session rotation does, so the finalization reaches the host. |
| An owed kickoff | Refuse: a turn-driven member that still owes an initial message is refused `SESSION_BUSY` / `fork_source_unavailable` / `running`, since the rotation terminal requires the kickoff quiesced. (Autonomous members are refused before this, by the `TurnDriven` rule.) |
| Forked participant attachments and live delegation sources | Refuse `UnsupportedForMode`, as respawn does. |
| Members forked from this one (a seated member whose `fork_source` names this identity and `S0`) | Refuse `UnsupportedForMode`, naming them: their overlay link follows the source only while it is bound to `S0` (`follow_fork_link`, `crates/meerkat-mob/src/runtime/fork_build.rs`), so a rewind would silently break it. The host retires them first. |
| Scheduled resumes of `S0` | While the hold stands, refused as other input is (below); after the archive, refused as an archived session. |

### Admission of turns and member work

`mob/turn_start` keeps its carrier (`turn/start`, which answers the turn's result and carries per-turn options); the generation check goes into the turn start's own admission. The handler resolves its target and checks the member in one actor-gated step: the identity's current generation, fence and session, and not `Retiring`.

A run-start hold holds runs, not input: a turn checked just before Begin would be queued in `S0`'s ledger behind the hold, and ended by the archive instead of refused, and could make the fork's busy guard fire. The rewind's hold therefore also **refuses new input** on `S0`, so nothing reaches `S0`'s ledger after the hold is staged. The refusal is a guard in the session runtime's input admission keyed on the new hold reason. The existing `ResolveAdmissionValidation` arms (`crates/meerkat-machine-schema/src/catalog/dsl/meerkat_machine.rs`) check only the input's own shape: durability, origin, kind and the peer fields the shell passes in. The new arm, `ResolveAdmissionValidationMemberRewindHeld`, reads a machine fact instead, `self.run_start_holds.contains(RunStartHoldReason::MemberRewind)`, so the input gains no field. Its guard is the accept arm's shape conditions **and** the hold; the accept arm gains the negated hold guard; the shape-rejection arms are untouched. So for an input that is both malformed and held, **the shape rejection wins**: it is the permanent fact, and a caller told only to retry would retry into a refusal. The arm rejects with a new `AdmissionRejectReasonKind::MemberRewindHeld`, for every input origin (operator, peer, external, flow, system). No other hold reason refuses input, so the existing holds keep queueing. Input admitted before the hold is seen by Begin's idle re-check, which refuses the rewind busy.

**The retryable class.** The rejections `MemberRewindHeld` joins are permanent today: the driver turns a validation reject into `RuntimeDriverError::ValidationFailed`, which `turn/start` answers `INVALID_PARAMS` (`crates/meerkat-rpc/src/session_runtime.rs`), which the comms drain resolves as the terminal `DurableAdmissionRejected` for an in-process sender (`crates/meerkat-comms/src/router.rs`), and which gives a network sender no ack (`crates/meerkat-comms/src/io_task.rs`). `MemberRewindHeld` is not a fact about the input, so the driver does not make it `ValidationFailed`: it maps it to a new `RuntimeDriverError::InputHeld { session_id, hold: RunStartHoldReason }`, a retryable class beside `DurableResumeHeld`, and each surface maps that:

| Surface | `InputHeld` becomes | A retry reaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `turn/start`, a session-targeted schedule, any session-addressed input door on `S0` | `SESSION_BUSY`, `data.kind = member_rewind_pending`, retryable | `SESSION_NOT_FOUND` after the commit (`S0` is archived), the same-session successor after the failure end |
| `mob/turn_start`, `mob/member_send` and other member-targeted admissions whose actor-gated check passed just before Begin and whose input reached the runtime after it | `MobError::LifecycleOperationPending`, the answer the actor-gated check gives once the intent stands | `S1` |
| An in-process peer envelope at `S0`'s ingress (the sender resolved the live route just before Begin closed it) | the drain does not resolve the claim as `Rejected`: it answers `IngressDeliveryOutcome::Dropped { reason: DropReason::InputHeld }` (a new variant; `DropReason` is `#[non_exhaustive]`), so the sender gets `SendError::AdmissionDropped { reason: InputHeld }`, nothing handed off and nothing in `S0`'s ledger; the `send` tool renders it as a retryable non-delivery. `InputHeld` stays inside comms: `From<DropReason> for AdmissionDropReason` (`crates/meerkat-comms/src/inbox.rs`) maps it onto `AdmissionDropReason::SessionClosed`, so the wire's `peer_admission_dropped` reason is `session_closed`, a token both SDKs already type and a sender already treats as not delivered and retries. The wire vocabulary is closed, which is the same reason this contract adds no `PeerWakeRefusal` token; the in-process distinction serves the drain's counters, logs and tests, where a held drop and a closed inbox are different causes | the retry finds the closed live route and takes the wake path (**Peers during the rotation**) |
| A network peer envelope at `S0`'s inbox, before step 4 | the same drop, which `io_task.rs` reports as `IoTaskError::IngressDropped(InputHeld)` with no ack, as it does for a closed inbox; the sender sees its ack timeout (`PeerOffline`), its existing retryable class. The protocol has no negative acknowledgement frame and this contract adds none | the member's unchanged endpoint, on `S1` after the commit |
| A network peer envelope after step 4 released `S0`'s runtime | no inbox: `DropReason::SessionClosed`, no ack, `PeerOffline` at the sender, as above | the same |

On the wire, then, a held drop and a closed inbox are the same `session_closed`, in process and over the network alike; the sender's handling is the same for both, so the collapse costs it nothing.

A network peer is told less than an in-process one (an ack timeout rather than a typed drop); that is the transport's existing shape, not a rewind rule, and the window is bounded by the fork. The answers by door:

| Door | Before the hold | Hold staged, rotation not committed | After the commit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `mob/turn_start`, `mob/member_send` and other member-targeted admissions | runs on `S0`; Begin then refuses the rewind busy | `MobError::LifecycleOperationPending`, retryable, the kind respawn uses for a retirement in flight. For a `CommittedFork` rotation, `SubmitWork`'s `Retiring` rejection answers this instead of member-not-found | targets `S1` |
| `mob/turn_start` resolved before the commit and started after it | | | `S0` answers `SESSION_NOT_FOUND` (`reject_archived_persisted_session_without_live`); the handler re-reads the member and maps it: the identity seated on another session answers `LifecycleOperationPending` (retry reaches `S1`), an identity gone answers `MemberNotFound`. The client never sees a session it did not name. |
| `turn/start` on `S0`, a scheduled resume of `S0` | runs; Begin refuses busy | `SESSION_BUSY`, `data.kind = member_rewind_pending`, retryable | `SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, as for any archived session |
| A peer message to `main` (sent to the member's endpoint) | handed to `S0`'s inbox, or wakes the member; Begin's idle re-check counts it and refuses the rewind busy | not delivered: `resume_gate_unavailable` on the wake route (below); at `S0`'s inbox, `AdmissionDropped { InputHeld }` in process or no ack over the network (**The retryable class**). Retryable, nothing handed off | reaches the successor through the member's unchanged endpoint and key: the send wakes it on `S1` and is delivered there under the same peer id. After the failure end it wakes the same-session successor on `S0`. |

**Peers during the rotation.** Begin closes `S0`'s live route to new envelopes, so an in-process send finds a closed route for a key with a wake route and takes the wake path (`inproc.rs`), before and after step 4 releases the runtime alike. The wake is decided by the machine: the DSL helper `member_wake_refusal` (`crates/meerkat-machine-schema/src/catalog/dsl/mob_machine.rs`) answers `Retiring` for any retiring member and `WakeMemberRefusedRunning` emits that answer in `MemberWakeClassified`; the two shell functions `member_wake_answer_from_state` and `member_wake_failure_from_state` (`crates/meerkat-mob/src/runtime/actor/member_wake.rs`) only report a closure the machine already made. `member_retiring` is not retryable, so a peer would drop a message for a member that continues. The helper therefore gains one fact, the member's rotation target, `self.member_rotation_targets.get_cloned(agent_identity)`, read next to the `Retiring` marker: a retiring member whose target is `CommittedFork` or `SameSession` answers `MemberWakeRefusalKind::ResumeGateUnavailable`, whose meaning already covers "a lifecycle operation on its session was still in flight"; a retiring member with target `FreshSession`, or with none (a plain retirement), keeps `Retiring`. `SameSession` takes the same answer because it has the same outcome: the member keeps its key and continues, and the rewind's failure end turns the rewind into one. Both shell functions stop reading `member_state_markers` on their own and call the generated helper over the same state reads, so the answer has one author and a change to it has one place. **Beyond the rewind:** the `SameSession` answer also changes what a peer gets during an ordinary same-session rotation (a `respawn_member`, a declared role migration, a runtime-mode restamp): `resume_gate_unavailable`, retryable, instead of `member_retiring`, which today makes a peer drop a message for a member that continues on the same session with the same key. That is a behaviour change to respawn in its own right, and it ships with its own changelog line and its own test (the respawn row under **Acceptance tests**), not folded into the rewind's. A `rotate_member` reset (`FreshSession`) and a plain retirement keep `member_retiring`. The peer then gets `PeerWakeRefusal::ResumeGateUnavailable`, retryable, with nothing handed off; the rewind adds no peer refusal token, since `PeerWakeRefusal` is a closed vocabulary every SDK parses. An envelope that reached `S0`'s inbox instead (the live route resolved just before Begin closed it, or network comms) meets the input guard, whose answers are in **The retryable class** above.

Nothing is retargeted, and nothing ran on `S0` after the hold.

### Streams

The notice is a new mob-level item, **`MemberIncarnationChanged`**, produced by **every rotation terminal**: the fresh-session terminals, the same-session terminals and the `CommittedFork` terminal (including the failure end). It is a machine effect beside the terminal's journal rows, not an `AgentEvent` variant. Its scope is rotations of a seated member; spawns, seats, retirements and destroys keep their existing lifecycle notices and `MemberSessionBindingChanged`, which is unchanged and still not emitted by a same-session terminal. The item serves respawn as well as rewind, since a respawn moves the member to a new runtime and generation on the same session.

**Opt-in, on its own method.** Both SDKs read `mob/stream_event`'s `event` and fall back to the whole params object when it is absent, and the per-member parsers then require `payload`. An item carried on `mob/stream_event` without `event` would therefore break every existing per-member subscriber at its first respawn or reset, including those that never rewind, and so would a stream that silently continued across rotations. Both are opt-in:

- `MobStreamOpenParams` gains `follow_rotations: bool`, default `false`. A stream opened without it is **byte-for-byte today's**: only `mob/stream_event` notifications with `event`, and a per-member stream that ends with the old incarnation, as now. It never carries the item. Because the params are `deny_unknown_fields`, a new client that asks for `follow_rotations` against an older server is refused `INVALID_PARAMS` instead of silently getting a stream without the item.
- On a stream opened with `follow_rotations: true`, the item travels on a **separate notification method, `mob/incarnation_changed`**, never on `mob/stream_event`:

```
mob/incarnation_changed { stream_id, sequence, incarnation_change: {
    agent_identity, member_ref,
    old_session_id, new_session_id,
    old_generation, new_generation,
    rotation: "fresh_session" | "same_session" | "committed_fork" } }
```

  `sequence` is the stream's next, so the item is ordered among the stream's events; there is no `epoch` (the item belongs to no session's sequence) and no top-level `session_id` or `event`. For `same_session`, `old_session_id` equals `new_session_id`. A separate method keeps even an opted-in stream decodable by a reader that does not know the item: neither SDK routes an unknown method without a top-level `session_id` to a subscriber.

**The consumer-facing rule:** a consumer that wants to follow a member across respawns, resets and rewinds opens its stream with `follow_rotations: true` and handles `mob/incarnation_changed`; every `mob/stream_event` it receives keeps today's shape. A consumer that does not opt in sees no change at all, and learns of a rotation from `mob/member_status` (`current_session_id`, generation, `rewound_from`) or `mob/events`, as today.

With `follow_rotations: true`:

- **Mob-wide.** The router forwards the item when the terminal commits. It attaches a member's stream only after an incarnation check: the stream it attaches must belong to the incarnation MobMachine binds now, so new-incarnation events never arrive before the item under the old runtime id.
- **Per-member.** On every rotation of the member the stream ends the old incarnation's source after its last event, yields the item, and attaches the successor at its next wake (it is Dormant), on the same `stream_id`. `MemberStreamEpoch` is reset at the item and set again at the successor's attach, so events from `S1` carry `S1`'s epoch.
- **Reattach.** No producer writes the item into `S1`'s retained sequence, and nothing is retained across a restart, so a client reattaching with an old-epoch cursor does not replay it. Such a client, and one with no open stream, learns of the switch from `mob/member_status` or `mob/events`.

The changelog announces the new parameter and method; no existing wire shape changes.

### Redo

After a rewind the current session holds only the prefix. Redo is `mob/rewind_member` with `source_session_id` set to a session of the identity's rewind chain, at its committed end (full redo) or any earlier boundary (partial redo). The same sequence runs: the hold and idle check are on the current session, which is the one replaced; the source is an archived session, read through the lineage-authorized reads.

- **The chain is machine state.** `member_rewind_chains[identity] = [{ session_id, source_session_id, message_count, prefix_digest, generation }]`, appended by the `CommittedFork` terminal and restored on replay from its rows; `prefix_digest` is the fork's `expected_prefix` digest, which guards **Source resolution**. The start step checks a requested source against it: it must be an entry's `session_id` or `source_session_id`, not the current session.
- **The chain is bounded.** At most **32 entries per identity**. The terminal that appends a 33rd evicts the oldest entry in the same step (first in, first out). A redo naming a session only the evicted entry held is refused `rewind_source_not_in_lineage`, and source resolution stops at the oldest kept entry and forks that session as it is. Each entry keeps an archived session readable through the lifted reads, so the bound also bounds what the lineage authorizes; 32 is far beyond an interactive host's undo depth and keeps `rewind_lineage` on `mob/member_status` small.
- **The boundary is the terminal kind.** The chain is kept across the same-session terminals (a respawn) and the committed-fork terminal, and dropped at every other terminal: a fresh-session rotation (how a host resets a member or deletes and recreates an identity, which discards the conversation), a retirement, a destroy. A mob reset (`MobHandle::reset`, `ResetToRunning`) clears every identity's chain, as it ends every identity. A predecessor's sessions, though bound to the same identity, are then refused `INVALID_PARAMS`, `data.kind = rewind_source_not_in_lineage`. The machine needs no creation ids for this; the creation id stays what it is today and plays no part in the chain.
- **Status reads never fold the journal.** `mob/member_status` carries `rewind_lineage`, newest first, from the roster's projection of the machine state.
- Re-seating a retired session as-is is not offered: it would bind one session to the identity in two generations. Every redo produces a fresh session.

### What carries over

The successor keeps the member's identity, creation id, profile, runtime mode, labels, spawner, wiring exemption, endpoint, edges and identity-scoped tool grants (subject `member(mob, incarnation, identity)`). An archive ends no tool grant: only an identity's end does (`end_member_identity_tool_grants`, over the sessions MobMachine holds for it then), and after the rewind `S0` is no longer among those. Step 4 therefore ends `S0`'s session-scoped grants explicitly; an end repeated changes nothing, so recovery may repeat it. They are not carried to `S1`: a session-scoped grant was a decision about `S0`'s conversation, and the host grants again on `S1` if it wants to. The roster's `fork_source` is unchanged and is never set to the member's own session; lineage lives only in `rewound_from` and the chain.

## Acceptance tests

In `crates/meerkat-rpc/tests/member_session_fork_at_boundary.rs` unless noted:

- `a_member_session_forks_at_a_message_boundary`, un-ignored on `mob/rewind_member`; `main` is Dormant on `S1` after the call, `S1` is in neither unborn set, and its next turn resumes `S1` with the prefix instead of creating a session.
- Rotation rows (`crates/meerkat-mob/src/runtime/tests/rotation_intent_rows.rs` siblings): the start row carries the `fork_rotation` record, with `expected_prefix`, under its own key; the terminal appends `MemberRetired` and `MemberSpawned` in one batch with `rewound_from` and the kept creation id; a rollback fixture decodes both with the 0.9.8 reader.
- Fork preflight: a source with a key copied from an earlier session at the boundary (`NotEffectiveInSource`), a boundary inside a fork's re-activation suffix, a pending input boundary, a split tool group, a prefix blob missing from the store, and a member key with no comms identity root each refuse at Begin with no start row and the hold released.
- Instruction state at the boundary: a key activated before the boundary is effective on `S1`, a key activated in the cut tail is not; a second rewind into the first rewind's copied prefix resolves to the earlier session and carries its activated key.
- Source resolution across a rewrite: rewind at k, compact `S1` (the copied prefix is rebuilt into a summary), then rewind at j < k. The digest no longer matches the chain entry, so the boundary is not mapped into `S0`: the result names `S1` as the source, the new session's prefix is `S1`'s first j messages as they are now, and a head copied from `S0` is refused `NotEffectiveInSource` before any start row. Without the compaction the same j resolves to `S0`.
- Normalized digest: a prefix holding an inline image is rewound; the recorded digest equals the child's `transcript_prefix_digest(message_count)`; a restart after the fork verifies the child, digest included, and does not take the failure end.
- Start shape: the `CommittedFork` start writes the same rotation session, retire-pending session, archive-only marker and started role as a same-session start on `S0`, and the restate changes only the target.
- Failure end: a fork refusal injected after the start row, and a planned child whose prefix digest conflicts (both right after the fork and on recovery), each end with the member Dormant on `S0` at the next generation, not `Retiring`, through `CommitMemberRotationSameSession`; the caller gets `rewind_not_committed`; the conflicting child is archived and has no key file; the chain gains no entry. The `MemberRotationRestated` row precedes the terminal pair, and a restart between the restate and the terminal drives the same-session terminal without a second fork; replay of the finished rows recovers no intent.
- Restart finishes forward: crash after the start row (no child: the fork runs again on the same planned id), after the fork (the child is observed and verified; no second fork, no read of an archived source), and after the archive; each restart ends on the same `S1`.
- Archived old session: after the rewind `S0` is archived, `turn/start` and a session-targeted schedule on it are refused, `session/history` still reads it, and the generic fork and session reads still refuse it while the lineage-authorized ones read it. A lifted fork read of an archived source whose projection is materialized from replay leaves the source's stored rows unchanged.
- Grants: a session-scoped grant on `S0` has ended after the rewind and does not apply on `S1`; an identity-scoped grant still applies on `S1`.
- Identity entry: archive an identity entry after a rewind and unarchive it; it is seated on `S1`, never `S0`.
- Wired peer: a peer wired explicitly with `wire()` sends to `main` before and after the rewind, and the second message lands on `S1` with the same peer id.
- Peer in each phase (paused after Begin, and again after step 4 released `S0`'s runtime): the peer's send is refused `resume_gate_unavailable`, retryable, nothing handed off and `S0`'s ledger unchanged; the same retry after the commit is delivered on `S1`; after the failure end it is delivered on `S0`'s same-session successor. A plain retirement still answers `member_retiring`. An in-process envelope that claimed `S0`'s ingress before Begin is answered `AdmissionDropped { InputHeld }`, not `DurableAdmissionRejected`, and its ingress row is not `Rejected`; its retry is refused `resume_gate_unavailable`. A network envelope at `S0`'s inbox gets no ack and leaves no `Rejected` row.
- Wake refusal (`mob_machine` tests): `member_wake_refusal` over a retiring member answers `ResumeGateUnavailable` with target `CommittedFork` or `SameSession` and `Retiring` with `FreshSession` or no target; `member_wake_answer_from_state` and `member_wake_failure_from_state` return the helper's answer for the same state.
- Respawn (its own change): a peer's send during an ordinary `respawn_member`, paused after its start row, is refused `resume_gate_unavailable`, retryable, nothing handed off; the retry after the terminal is delivered on the same session under the same peer id. A `rotate_member` reset and a `retire_member` still answer `member_retiring`.
- Drop reason on the wire (`meerkat-comms` tests): a drain that meets `InputHeld` resolves the delivery `Dropped { InputHeld }`, the sender's `SendError::AdmissionDropped` carries `InputHeld`, and the error's wire payload carries `session_closed`; `AdmissionDropReason` gains no variant and the SDK types are unchanged.
- Owed-work refusals: a run waiting on an approval decision, a pending detached job, a queued input, an owed kickoff, a forked participant attachment and a member forked from `main` each refuse the rewind with nothing started.
- Input guard (`meerkat-runtime` machine tests): with `MemberRewind` held, inputs of every origin are rejected `MemberRewindHeld`; an input that is also malformed is rejected for its shape; with only `HostPreparation` held, input is still queued. The driver surfaces the reject as `InputHeld`, not `ValidationFailed`.
- Turn admission races (`crates/meerkat-mob` runtime tests, paused after Begin): a `mob/turn_start` and a `mob/member_send` are refused `LifecycleOperationPending`, a `turn/start` on `S0` is refused busy, and `S0`'s ledger gains no input; a `mob/turn_start` resolved before the commit and started after it answers `LifecycleOperationPending`, not `SESSION_NOT_FOUND`; a `turn/start` whose runtime admission met the hold answers `SESSION_BUSY` / `member_rewind_pending`, not `INVALID_PARAMS`; nothing ran on `S0` after the hold.
- Redo boundary: rewind, respawn, rewind again; redo from the first chain entry succeeds. Rewind, then `rotate_member` (a reset), and redo from the chain is refused `rewind_source_not_in_lineage`; the same after retire and recreate, and after a mob reset; `rewind_lineage` is empty in each. The same after a restart.
- Chain bound: 33 rewinds leave 32 entries; a redo from the evicted session is refused `rewind_source_not_in_lineage`, also after a restart.
- Streams by default: a mob-wide and a per-member stream opened without `follow_rotations` across a rewind, a respawn and a `rotate_member` receive exactly today's notifications (no `mob/incarnation_changed`, every `mob/stream_event` with `event`), and the per-member stream ends with the old incarnation; both SDKs' existing per-member readers decode them without error.
- Streams with `follow_rotations: true`, both forms: a subscriber gets `mob/incarnation_changed` (rotation `committed_fork`, old and new session and generation) before any `S1` event; the per-member stream stays on its `stream_id` and carries `S1`'s epoch; a `respawn_member` yields `same_session` with equal session ids, and a `rotate_member` yields `fresh_session`; a reattach with an old-epoch cursor learns the switch from `mob/member_status`. SDK tests: a reader that does not know the method routes it to no subscriber and keeps decoding the stream.
- Restrictions: the deny list and the host dispatch admission (#2112 lineage case) hold on `S1`, and a rebuild emits no fork-source warning.

## Consumers
