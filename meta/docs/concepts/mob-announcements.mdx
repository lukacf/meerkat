Mob announcements carry developments to the members that asked for them. The
host, or a member through its tools, announces a development to a mob. Every
member subscription that names that mob and one of the announcement's topics
captures it, and each subscriber receives what it is owed in bounded batches.
Each batch is one Meerkat host continuation keyed by its batch id.
Announcements also come from [sources](#sources): a collector that observes
an external system, such as a mailbox or a calendar, reports what changed,
and each change becomes one announcement.

Mob announcements are optional, and a persistent, identity-first host
enables them the same way on every surface: `rpc_gateway` with
`runtime_options.mob_announcements = {"enabled": true}`, `meta_gateway` with
its `mob_announcements` init param, and a Rust host with
`UnifiedRuntimeBuilder::mob_announcements`. Each refuses a configuration it
cannot compose before it builds anything. The batch, retention and limit
options are in [Configuration](/reference/configuration). Without them,
nothing opens the announcement database (`mob_announcements.sqlite3`), and
the methods answer `mob_announcements_unavailable`.

The console serves the same methods on `/console/rpc`. Every one of them,
the reads included, needs the `runtime.admin` grant, and a read-only console
refuses the ones that change state.

## Announcements

An announcement belongs to one mob hosted on the gateway, and its source key
identifies it within that mob. Announcing the same key with the same content
again replays the first announcement; the same key with other content is
refused as a conflict. An announcement names its topics and its body, and can
carry a correlation id and the announcements that caused it. Batch bodies
quote each announcement and name its mob and its announcer.

## Subscriptions

A subscription belongs to one member, `{mob_id, identity}`, and names the
topics it wants from the mobs it names (the gateway's own mob by default). It
captures announcements from now on, or from a frontier of per-mob sequence
numbers. Pause, resume and cancel name the revision they expect, so a stale
caller is refused instead of overwriting a newer change.

A subscriber can be:

- a registered identity of the gateway's mob that the gateway's runtime serves;
- a member of another mob hosted on the gateway, such as a mob a member
  created, subscribed by the host by its plain roster id;
- a member of a mob on a peer gateway, reached through
  [keyed inject](/concepts/access-control#keyed-inject) when this gateway's
  contact directory files that mob with a pinned key and this gateway holds a
  signing key.

Members announce and subscribe for themselves through the `mob_announcements`
tool bundle (`mob_announce` and `mob_subscription`) when their profile lists
it in `tools.rust_bundles`. A member of a mob a member created gets them only
under the host's `member_tools_in_child_mobs` ceiling, by naming
`mob_announcements` in its own profile's `tools.bundles`. A member announces
into its own mob as itself and manages only its own subscriptions. It may name
its own mob and the mobs above and below it among the mobs members created,
never a sibling's. Each call is attributed to exactly one member or refused,
within per-member limits.

## Delivery

- An announcement is captured for every matching subscription in the same
  transaction that records it.
- A lost acknowledgment is reconciled from the native status, without a second
  submission. A batch is submitted again only after a signed answer that
  nothing was committed.
- A delivery stranded on a retired incarnation moves to a successor batch that
  names its predecessor.
- What waited past the retention window for a delivery that never started
  expires, and the member's next batch reports it as a gap. A batch whose
  outcome, or whose admission, was never confirmed settles without being
  submitted again, and the next batch reports its announcements as not
  confirmed.
- Reading a batch shows the native status and the admitted input's runtime
  state. Admission is not processing: the input's state says what the
  runtime did with it.
- Meta also reads what became of each admitted batch's turn: `handled`,
  `handled_unverified` (consumed, with no receipt that says how),
  `unhandled` with its cause, or `unknown`.
- A policy decides what happens to the items of an unhandled turn. They
  can be redelivered first in the member's next batch, under a notice
  that says what happened. They can be held for an operator. Or the
  outcome is only reported. Source items are redelivered by default, at
  most twice, and plain announcements are reported, as before.
- Processing is at least once. A turn interrupted after partial progress
  (a cancelled run, a retired member, a stop that ends the turn) is
  unhandled even when its batch, or part of what the turn did, already
  reached the member's transcript. When the policy redelivers its items,
  they come again under the notice that what the turn started may already
  have happened, at most twice per item; the hold policy keeps them for an
  operator instead.
- An operator redelivers or dismisses what a batch holds.

## When a subscription ends

A subscription ends, with a typed reason, only on a final fact:

- every mob it named was destroyed;
- for a member of another mob hosted here, that mob was destroyed, created
  again, completed or reset after the subscription was made;
- a peer gateway answered, signed, that its member departed. No gateway of
  this release records a final fact that a member left, so none gives that
  answer yet.

A member that is not registered, or not served where its deliveries go, ends
nothing: it may be registered or served again. Neither does a member that
left this gateway, is governed, or has no live incarnation. Its deliveries
wait, and the subscription's `marker` says why and on what evidence (a
verified signed answer, an unverified one, or this gateway's own fact). Its
owed batches expire after the retention window, and `subscriptions/cancel`
ends a subscription whose member is not coming back.

## Sources

A source is a durable record in one mob for one external scope that a
collector observes: a mailbox, a calendar, a channel. The host declares it
with `sources/put` and names the provider, the account the collector reads
as (the source's principal), the scope, the topics it owns, who may receive
its items (its audience) and how they reach them (its routes). A collector,
which the host runs or schedules, reads the provider from the source's
cursor and reports each step with `sources/advance`: the items it observed
and the next cursor, in one transaction. Meta derives each item's source key
from the source's principal and scope and the item's stream, id, version and
change, and announces it as `{"kind": "source", "source_id": ...}`. Every
version of an item, and its deletion, is its own announcement; the same
version collected again replays instead of announcing twice, and other
content under the same key is recorded as a conflict and never stops the
source.

Announcements carry each observed change once to each subscribed member. An
agent that also polls the provider through its own tools sees the same items
again; give such agents either announcements or polling for a scope, not
both. Forwarding by a triage agent is the agent's judgement within the
forward audience. Announcement bodies are references or summaries: the full
content lives in the app's own index or another store and is fetched under
the reader's own authority. Announcements are retained 7 days and are not a
search index or a store. Audience labels are set when a member is spawned
and change only by a respawn. Widening a forward audience trusts the triage
agent's redaction.

### Collecting

- Each step names the source revision and the cursor version it read. A
  configuration change between the read and the step moves the revision,
  and the step is refused with the source's current position
  (`stale_revision`); another collector's step that committed first moves
  the version (`source_version_conflict`). Neither commits anything, and
  the collector reads `sources/get` and starts again from there.
- Each step carries an operation id. Retrying the same id with the same
  request returns the original answer (`replayed`), so a lost response is
  safe to retry; a step built from another read takes a new id.
- The cursor token is the adapter's own: Meta stores it as given, never
  parses or logs it, and returns it only from `sources/get` and in the data
  of the refusals above.
- A failure the collector cannot get past is reported with
  `sources/record_failure`, fenced the same way, and counts in the source's
  health.
- A paused source takes no step and keeps its cursor. Ending a source ends
  its generation; declaring it again starts the next generation from a
  fresh baseline, and the ended generation's topics stay reserved to its
  source id.

### Onboarding

A new source first takes the provider's position without announcing its
history (`baselining`), or announces a bounded history first
(`backfilling`, at most 1,000 items); it is `live` once the final page of
that read commits. Streams, the containers inside one scope such as one
calendar of an account, onboard the same way. When the provider cannot
continue from the cursor, a step that rebaselines records a gap and
announces one gap notice on the source's topics, so subscribers learn that
changes may be missing between two times instead of receiving a flood.

### Who receives source items

A source owns its topics in its mob: another source cannot claim them, and a
plain announce on one is refused. Its audience grants name members, roles
or labels of the roster, and a member outside it can neither subscribe to
those topics nor receive their items. Its routes say how members reach the
items: a direct route lets members subscribe to its topics, and a triage
route hands them to one triage member, which may announce on the route's
forward topics to the forward audience. One member is never reachable both
ways for one source, and the members that own a live conversation on the
source's scope are never recipients.

Coverage is checked when a member subscribes, when an item is captured,
when its batch is sealed and immediately before every native submission.
An item whose member no longer passes is held, never sealed or dropped,
and is released only by a fresh check: held items are rechecked at least
every 5 minutes; an unreadable roster decides nothing; on a peer route a
failing last check waits out the quiet window, then reads status before
cancelling. Items held past the retention window expire without a gap line
to their member. On a gateway whose runtimes are not governed, the last
check before submission is the last point Meta controls: a revocation after
Meerkat committed a batch into the member's inbox is not recalled, and
`audit/list` counts it as `delivered_across_revocation`. Every refusal, withholding, hold, release
and expiry is a counted audit entry.

### Detecting support

A gateway that serves sources lists `mob_announcements.sources.v1` in
`meta/capabilities.feature_capabilities`, and its `mob_announcements`
object says whether deliveries can reach members locally. A gateway that
serves mob announcements without sources lists their methods but not that
capability, and an older one answers the source methods as unknown
methods. The SDKs' `supports_announcement_sources` (Python) and
`supportsAnnouncementSources` (TypeScript) read the capability.

## Declarations

A host can keep its sources and subscriptions as declarations instead of
putting each one: in its options, inline or as a packaged sidecar file, or
applied at runtime with `declarations/apply`. A declaration names a
revision. Meta applies a higher one and refuses a lower one, reconciles at
startup before it starts delivering, and changes only the records a
declaration made, so records made at runtime and an operator's pause, end
or cancel stand. What the runtime refuses for now, such as a member that
has not registered yet, waits as pending and is retried;
`declarations/get` shows each declared record as applied, pending,
suppressed or left.

A subscription can name every member of a mob with a role instead of one
member: a role template. Each member with the role gets its own child
subscription at the first announcement the template matches, so members
spawned later are covered, and a member that loses the role stops
capturing at once and still receives what it was owed.

## Health and escalations

A source or member that needs a person's attention raises an escalation. An
escalation is a durable record, and only one is open per cause and subject.

- A source escalates when its collections keep failing, when it stops
  advancing because its collector is not running, and when it reports a gap
  or a conflict. If its health policy opts in, it also escalates when it
  stays quiet or its snapshots stay empty.
- A member escalates when its deliveries have waited for an hour, when its
  batches report gaps, and when items are held because its coverage changed.
  It also escalates when it holds items for an operator after an unhandled
  turn, and when its turns keep ending with no receipt that says how.
- A declared record that stays pending escalates as well.

An escalation stays open while its condition holds. While it is open, it is
notified again every day by default, and it resolves when the condition
ends.

The host hears of every raise, notification and resolution through its
`meta/on_error` hook. After a reconnect, `escalations/list` reads the events
it missed, from its last cursor. Agents hear of escalations on the reserved
topic `mob_announcements.health`. A member that subscribes to it receives:

- the escalations of the sources whose audience covers it;
- the member escalations, when the host's `health_audience` covers it.

Several reads show the same state:

- `members/get` and `members/list` read a member's backlog, holds, suspend
  marker and open escalations.
- A source's health reads its open escalations and whether this gateway
  schedules its runner.
- `meta/status` counts them.
